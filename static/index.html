Why does demand affect warehouse placement? (Mathematical Principle)

GRIDPOINT minimizes Total Geographic Delivery Distance weighted by demand ($\sum \text{demand}_i \times \text{distance}_{ij}$). High-demand delivery zones exert far greater mathematical pull on warehouse location than low-demand zones:

High-Demand Zone (e.g. Koramangala)
420 orders/day × 2.4 km = 1,008 order-km
Reducing distance here yields massive delivery cost and fleet savings.
Low-Demand Zone (e.g. Kengeri)
150 orders/day × 14.0 km = 2,100 order-km
Lower order volume reduces its influence on optimal warehouse placement.

Mathematical Formulations & Honesty Rules
Transparent engineering standards, exact optimization models, and AI usage disclosure.

1. Capacitated Facility Location Problem (CFLP) Integer Program

Let $I$ be the set of delivery zones with demands $d_i$, $J$ candidate warehouse locations with throughput capacities $C_j$, and $c_{ij}$ the geographic distance between zone $i$ and site $j$:

Minimize: Z = ∑ (i ∈ I) ∑ (j ∈ J) d_i · c_{ij} · x_{ij} + ∑ (j ∈ J) F_j · y_j
Subject to:
  • ∑ (j ∈ J) x_{ij} = 1, ∀ i ∈ I   (Single assignment: every zone served)
  • ∑ (i ∈ I) d_i · x_{ij} ≤ C_j · y_j, ∀ j ∈ J   (Capacity constraints)
  • ∑ (j ∈ J) y_j = p   (Facility cardinality: exactly p open sites)
  • c_{ij} · x_{ij} ≤ R_{max}, ∀ i, j   (Service radius feasibility)
  • x_{ij} ∈ {0, 1}, y_j ∈ {0, 1}

2. Product Honesty & Transparency Rules

  • Geographic Distance: Distances represent straight-line Haversine arcs, not routing turn-by-turn road measurements.
  • Reference Baseline: Every comparison explicitly compares against the 1-median Single-Warehouse Reference Baseline.
  • Terminology: The system models aggregate "delivery zones" and "neighborhoods", never individual consumer orders.
  • Solver Integrity: "Optimal" is displayed only when proven by CBC Branch-and-Cut; heuristic solutions are labeled "Feasible (Heuristic)".

4. Public Geodata Source & Attribution (Honesty Disclosure)

Real city and town coordinates used in network sampling are sourced from the open-source dr5hn/countries-states-cities-database (mirrored on Kaggle as liewyousheng/geolocation) under the Open Database License (ODbL) v1.0.
Sampler Architecture: No model call hallucinates coordinates or names. In regional mode, distinct real towns are sampled directly from this verified public dataset. In single-city mode, derived demonstration zones are jittered within 2–8 km of a real city anchor. Order volumes and capacity requirements are synthesized using deterministic, non-uniform statistical distributions. The LLM is used strictly to parse plain-language instructions into structured configuration parameters.
